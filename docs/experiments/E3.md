# E3 — Which class of disagreement can the comparison stage reach?

**Status: DESIGNED, NOT RUN.** The fixture exists and the deterministic baseline
is recorded below. No model has seen `claim_002`.

This document is written **before** the run on purpose. E2's recommendation was
"do not build the graph layer", and the request that prompted E3 was to build it.
A prediction written after seeing the result is not a prediction, and an
experiment whose hypothesis is adjustable after the fact cannot overturn a prior
decision or confirm one.

---

## 1. The question

E2 closed with two conditions for revisiting the graph layer:

> *"Revisit only if a fixture requires multi-hop reasoning across entities, or if
> relationships need lifecycle independent of the assertions that produced them."*

E3 asks the first one directly:

> **Is there a class of real disagreement in a claim file that the flat
> `groupby(subject_entity_id, event_id, predicate)` comparison cannot reach?**

If yes, the grouping key is the limit rather than the model, and a richer
representation has a job. If no, E2 stands and the flat model keeps its win.

---

## 2. What the fixture is

`data/fixtures/claim_002`, gold at `data/gold/claim_002.gold.json`. Fully
synthetic, no objective anchor, authored at **C3**: FNOL, a recorded statement,
and an independent account. Two handlers, a reassignment, and a reserve increase,
so it sits inside the D-018 segment the product is for.

Three differences are planted, in two classes.

| | Subject | Positions | Class | Shares a grouping key? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `gold_d_001` | travel_direction | FNOL *westbound* vs statement *eastbound* | **Flat control** | Yes — (vehicle_1, collision, travel_direction) |
| `gold_d_002` | road_surface | FNOL *dry* vs independent *wet* | **Flat control** | Yes — (roadway_1, collision, road_surface) |
| `gold_d_003` | second vehicle's motion | statement *reversed out of a parking space* vs independent *remained parked the whole time* | **Cross-predicate** | **No** — `maneuver` vs `unit_type` |

The third is the treatment. Both positions are about `vehicle_2`, both are
verified, both are plainly incompatible to any reader — and they land on
different predicates, so no `groupby` key contains both and no comparison is ever
attempted. The vocabulary separates them deliberately: the note on `maneuver`
records that `parked` is "a maneuver value AND a unit type; both are recorded
rather than merged." That separation is right for extraction and is exactly what
hides this conflict.

Six traps are also planted, in `differences_must_not_be_reported`. They matter as
much as the conflicts: a system that finds `gold_d_003` by comparing everything
to everything would also report all six.

* `occupant_count` 1 vs `occupant_observation` none_observed — a failure to
  observe is not an absence. The vocabulary separates these on purpose.
* `approximate_speed` 25 (vehicle) vs `posted_speed_limit` 20 (roadway) — never
  comparable; reporting it would manufacture a conduct finding.
* `contact_point` front on vehicle_1 vs right_side on vehicle_2 — one coherent
  geometry, two separate entities.
* Street lighting — the claimant does not recall, and non-recall takes no position.
* Rain vs wet surface — corroboration.
* Damage band — single source. Silence is not a position.

**The traps and the treatment are the same shape.** Both are two verified
assertions on one entity under different predicates. The only thing separating
"should be reported" from "must not be" is whether the two predicates describe
one property of the world. That is the finding below.

## 3. What this fixture could NOT be made to test

The original intent was a **multi-hop** conflict — two assertions about different
entities, contradictory only when a relation between the entities is traversed.
It cannot be authored in data alone, and the reason is structural.

**None of the 26 predicates takes another entity as its value.** Every one is
`(entity, attribute, scalar)`. There is no `operated_by`, no `driver_of`, no
`occupant_of`. The link that would carry the hop — the claimant drives vehicle_1
— exists only as English prose inside a roster `description`
(`claim_mapping.py:72`, "The claimant, driver of vehicle_1"). It is not data.
Nothing can traverse it, and nothing can be asserted about it by two sources.

So E2's first revisit condition cannot be met by writing a fixture. A multi-hop
conflict requires **relation predicates first**, and a relation with its own
identity and its own value is the first brick of the representation E2
recommended against. The two possible readings:

* **The condition is unreachable as written**, and should be restated as "a
  fixture requires relation predicates to express a real conflict."
* **Or the honest multi-hop cases in this domain are the ones V1 forbids
  anyway.** The cross-entity contradictions available with this vocabulary —
  two rear impacts in a two-vehicle collision, a speed above a posted limit —
  need physical or conduct inference. V1 has no `Inference` type, by decision
  (D-027 and the domain model). A graph that enabled them would enable the thing
  the product declines to do.

This is a finding, not a blocked task. It makes E2's recommendation **stronger**:
the graph layer is not waiting on a fixture, it is waiting on a reason.

## 4. Prediction, recorded before the run

1. **`gold_d_001` and `gold_d_002` are found.** Both share a grouping key with
   two distinct normalised values. E1b found its one planted difference in 3 of
   3 runs, and these are the same shape.
2. **`gold_d_003` is not found, in any run, by either arm of E2's design.** Not
   because the model fails to see it — it is never shown both positions
   together. Arm A puts all facts in one call and could in principle notice, but
   it has no neighbourhood to attach the finding to; Arm B never co-locates them.
3. **Zero of the six traps are reported.** If any are, the fixture has found a
   precision problem that matters more than the recall question.

**If prediction 2 is wrong** — if a run finds `gold_d_003` — then the flat model
is better than its grouping key suggests and E2's recommendation needs no help.
**If prediction 2 holds**, the next question is *not* "build the graph layer." It
is: is a cross-predicate conflict worth catching, and who decides which predicate
pairs describe one property? That is a materiality-adjacent judgement, which is
gated on the §12 study (D-014). A predicate-pair table authored by us would be
us deciding what conflicts matter, which is the one thing this project does not
do.

## 5. Deterministic baseline, as run

`--client null`, no model, 7 of 9 stages:

```
stage counts   passages 7 · facts_deterministic 14 · facts_extracted 0 · facts_dropped 0
fact_recall                 0.483   (14 of 29 — the 15 extracted facts await a model)
fact_precision              1.000
hallucination_rate          0.000
timeline_ordering_accuracy  1.000
open_question_recall        1.000
difference_recall           0.000   (no model ran; both controls are extracted)
```

`fact_precision 1.000` is the meaningful number: the deterministic half of the
gold was rewritten to match what the stage actually emits, after a first draft
expected facts no stage produces. Three corrections came out of that:

* **The listed-driver check is a difference, not a fact** — and it fires only
  when the driver is *absent* from the policy. Here the driver is listed, so its
  correct output is silence. It moved to `differences_must_not_be_reported`.
* **A missing police report is correctly not flagged.** The checklist treats
  `independent_account` and `police_report` as alternatives
  (`checklist.py:35`), and this fixture has an independent account.
* **A missing repair estimate is correctly not flagged at C3** — it is expected
  from C4.

All three were my gold being wrong and the implementation being right.

## 6. Two defects found while building this

### 6.1 `open_question_recall` does not discriminate between documents

The frozen metric matches a gold question to a produced one by one-directional
token overlap: at least half of the gold question's words longer than three
characters must appear in the produced question
(`metrics.py:_overlaps`). Every `expected_document_absent` question comes from one
template — *"The file does not contain X. Has it been requested or received?"* —
so the shared scaffolding alone clears the threshold and the discriminating word
is outvoted.

Demonstrated: the produced *adjuster notes* question matches the gold *police
report* question, while failing to match a correctly-worded *adjuster notes*
gold. On `claim_001`, **every** gold question matches **every** produced
question. Its reported `open_question_recall = 1.0` is correct, but only because
both expected questions were in fact produced — not because the metric
distinguished them. The same figure would survive a system that asked about
adjuster notes twice and never mentioned the independent account.

This is the **same defect in the same shape** as the one already fixed in the
forbidden-fact detector, where one-directional containment let shared scaffolding
outvote the single word carrying the meaning. That fix moved to symmetric
Jaccard. `metrics.py` is frozen, so per the D-037 precedent the correction lives
in the non-frozen reporting layer as `report.rescore_open_questions`, and it does
better than a threshold: `expected_source` is a discriminating key both sides
already carry, so the match needs no fuzzy comparison. Prose similarity is the
fallback, and symmetric when used. Five tests pin it, including one that asserts
the frozen metric still mis-credits — so if the freeze is ever changed, the
re-scorer's justification has to be rewritten rather than quietly kept.

### 6.2 `unresolved_by_file` is declared and never produced

The kind appears in `open_question.schema.json` and in the gold schema. **No code
emits it.** It was removed from `claim_002`'s gold, because expecting an output
no stage can produce caps the metric below 1.0 forever with no path to
improvement — measuring a gap in the contract rather than the system.

It is also the question this fixture most wants to ask: *whether the second
vehicle was stationary or reversing is not resolved by any document in the file.*
That is the honest output for `gold_d_003` — not a difference between two
sources, but an unresolved state of the file. Worth noting that the output type
for the treatment case already exists in the contract and has no producer.

### 6.3 E2 has no freeze manifest

`e2_ontology/ab_summary.json` records runs, model, arms and stage counts, but no
file digests, and `vocabulary.py` is not among E1's 22 frozen files. So the
ontology could change with nothing recording that E2's numbers came from a
different one. E1 and E1b are protected; E2 is not. Any change to the vocabulary
should be preceded by pinning the current digests, and such a pin is retroactive
— it records today's state, not a verified-at-run-time digest.

## 7. What would change the recommendation

Unchanged from E2, with one addition.

* A fixture that needs **relation predicates** to express a real conflict the
  product is permitted to report. §3 argues this may not exist in V1's scope.
* Relationships needing **lifecycle independent of the assertions** — the
  realistic version is a human-marked difference that must survive re-extraction
  of the documents that produced it. Nothing requires this yet.
* Scale past roughly a few thousand assertions, where `groupby` stops being free.
  Current: 46.

Nothing in E3 as designed meets any of these. The graph *view* shipped because
the DTO already carried the adjacency; the graph *layer* still has no job.
